# Contextual AI-style review for academic prose

Source basis: [Wikipedia:Signs of AI writing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing), consulted 2026-09-20. The source describes possible indicators, not proof, and warns against relying on detectors alone. Apply only the parts relevant to academic prose.

## High-value signals

Prioritize these because they often correspond to real editorial weaknesses regardless of provenance:

1. **Generic significance claims** — assertions that a result is pivotal, important, transformative, or part of a broader trend without specifying the mechanism or evidence.
2. **Superficial analysis** — an accurate statement followed by an unsupported participial phrase such as "highlighting," "underscoring," "reflecting," or "contributing to."
3. **Vague attribution** — "researchers argue," "it is widely recognized," or "critics note" without a traceable source or defined population.
4. **Promotional language** — inflated adjectives, claims of novelty, or claims of impact that exceed the design or cited evidence.
5. **Outline-like conclusions** — formulaic lists of challenges, opportunities, implications, and future directions that do not follow from the paper's results.
6. **Citation mismatch** — a cited source does not support the attached interpretation, or a reference is incomplete, unverifiable, or fabricated.
7. **Smoothing away specifics** — concrete institutional, historical, or empirical details are replaced by broadly applicable abstractions.

## Medium-value signals

Use these only when clustered or inconsistent with the author profile:

- Dense recurrence of fashionable words such as `delve`, `landscape`, `nuanced`, `robust`, `multifaceted`, `foster`, `underscore`, or `pivotal`.
- Repeated vague relationship verbs such as `aligns with`, `resonates with`, `is linked to`, or `reflects` without explaining how.
- Repeated negative parallelisms: `not only X but also Y`, `not merely X but Y`, or `Y rather than X`.
- Habitual groups of exactly three claims or adjectives.
- Uniform sentence and paragraph rhythm, especially when every paragraph follows claim-explanation-implication.
- Avoidance of simple `is` and `are` constructions in favor of inflated alternatives.
- Excessive em dashes, parenthetical asides, or polished transition phrases when these depart from the user's corpus.

## Weak or inapplicable signals

Do not flag these on their own:

- One vocabulary item or one em dash.
- Correctly formatted headings, tables, or Markdown.
- A formal register expected by the target journal.
- Non-native-English constructions that remain clear and are present in the user's established writing.
- Wikipedia-specific markup, edit-summary, user-page, or article-notability patterns.

## How to recommend revisions

Fix the underlying problem rather than swapping synonyms. Ask which precise result, mechanism, comparison, actor, or citation supports the sentence. Prefer deleting unsupported significance language to decorating it differently. Vary rhythm only when it improves the argument or restores a demonstrated feature of the author's voice.

Never present a checklist match as evidence that a passage was generated by AI.
